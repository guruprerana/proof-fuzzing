You are expanding a compact implication skeleton into a verifier-oriented proof artifact.

Input:
  problem_statement:
  {{PROBLEM_STATEMENT}}

  proof_text:
  {{PROOF_TEXT}}

  skeleton:
  {{SKELETON}}

Goal:
  Convert the skeleton into a modular claim-by-claim format that an LLM verifier can check locally.

Output sections:
  1. global context
  2. optional lemma modules L1, L2, ...
  3. main proof claims C1, C2, C3, ...


Global context rules:
  Put only shared definitions, fixed objects, and reusable notation in global context.

  Include:
    objects from the problem statement;
    notation introduced by the proof and used in multiple claims;
    parameterized notation such as "for each k, I_k denotes ...".

  Do not include:
    conclusions that require proof;
    lemmas;
    asymptotic estimates;
    final theorem statements, unless they are part of the problem target.


Claim format:
  claim C1:
  check_type: definition / arithmetic / modular arithmetic / counting / geometry / asymptotic estimate / infinitude / final assembly / other
  variables:
    explicit declarations for every free variable used in this claim.
  hypotheses:
    assumptions needed for this claim instance.
  allowed_references:
    structured references to earlier claims or exported lemma statements that may be used.
  conclusion:
    the local conclusion.


Lemma module format:
  lemma L1_descriptive_name:
  statement:
    the exported lemma statement.
  dependencies:
    global context only, or earlier exported lemmas if genuinely needed.
  proof_claims:
    claim L1.C1:
    check_type: ...
    variables:
      explicit declarations for every free variable used in this internal claim.
    hypotheses:
      assumptions needed for this internal claim instance.
    allowed_references:
      earlier internal claims L1.C*, global context, or exported earlier lemmas.
    conclusion:
      local conclusion.

    claim L1.final:
    check_type: final assembly
    variables:
      same universal variables as the lemma statement.
    hypotheses:
      hypotheses of the lemma statement.
    allowed_references:
      internal proof claims needed to prove the statement.
    conclusion:
      the exported lemma statement.
  exports:
    L1.statement.


Module rules:
  1. If the source proof states and proves a reusable lemma, represent it as a lemma module.
  2. Internal proof claims of a lemma should use namespaced labels such as L1.C1, L1.C2, ...
  3. Main proof claims should not depend on internal lemma claims unless the source proof explicitly reuses an internal fact.
  4. Main proof claims should normally reference only L1.statement instantiated with specific variables.
  5. A lemma statement is an interface. Its proof_claims verify the interface; later claims use the interface.


Meaning of allowed_references:
  The verifier may assume these earlier claims are already proved.
  The verifier should not use arbitrary earlier proof text unless it appears in hypotheses, global context, or allowed_references.
  If a reference is used with particular variables, write the instantiation explicitly.
  If a proved lemma is used, reference only its exported statement with a structured entry:
    - ref: L1.statement
      instantiation: H = G; M = 18n; K = 6n

Parseable allowed_references format:
  Use one of these forms exactly:

  allowed_references:
    none

  allowed_references:
    - ref: C2
      instantiation: k = current k; n = witness from C5
    - ref: L1.statement
      instantiation: H = G; M = 18n; K = K_1

  Rules:
    - Each referenced claim or exported statement gets its own "- ref:" entry.
    - Use stable ids such as C7, L1.C3, L1.final, or L1.statement.
    - Do not write vague references such as "previous estimates", "the tree lemma", or "claims above".
    - Do not use ranges such as "C1 through C10"; list each reference explicitly.
    - If no reference is used, write exactly "none".


Core requirements:
  1. Each claim should be checkable locally from its variables, hypotheses, global context, and allowed_references.
  2. Preserve the proof strategy of the source proof and the dependency structure of the skeleton.
  3. Do not turn local lemmas into global assumptions.
  4. Do not hide quantifier reasoning in vague aggregator claims.
  5. Split claims when one claim would require two different kinds of reasoning.
  6. Add small bridge claims when the skeleton uses an implicit equality, monotonicity fact, positivity fact, or distinctness fact.
  7. Use source-faithful mathematics. Do not repair the proof by inventing a different argument.
  8. Prefer explicit hypotheses over relying on the verifier to infer them.
  9. Preserve modularity: do not flatten a proved reusable lemma into the main proof unless the original proof did not isolate it and flattening is clearer.
  10. Do not expose lemma-internal proof claims as main-proof dependencies unless absolutely necessary.
  11. Ignore dataset metadata lines and final-answer wrappers unless they contain mathematical proof content.
  12. For positional numeral proofs, introduce quotient or remainder variables explicitly and handle the highest digit separately when a recurrence only applies below the highest digit.
  13. Preserve endpoint splits such as 0 <= i <= k-2 plus i = k-1 as separate local claims before aggregating them.
  14. If a claim reuses a construction proved under a WLOG ordering assumption, expose the relabeling or symmetry argument as its own claim before referencing the construction.
  15. For integer intervals, distinguish cardinality from real length before using residue-class coverage or pigeonhole-style interval arguments.
  16. In counting estimates, explicitly handle special counted objects such as 0 or 1, duplicate representations, and endpoint conventions; add a harmless constant term when the source proof absorbs one.
  17. When infinitude follows from constructed witnesses, include a distinctness or unboundedness claim showing the witnesses cannot collapse to finitely many objects.
  18. For Vieta-jumping arguments, declare the other root, prove it is a positive integer solution, and state the exact minimality comparison used before deriving a contradiction or bound.
  19. For finite case analysis after an inequality bound, either list the boundary cases or mark the claim's check_type as finite enumeration with enough hypotheses to audit all integer cases.
  20. For explicit existence examples after lower-bound elimination, include direct witness verification rather than relying only on a recurrence or construction description.
  21. When a divisibility condition is converted into an integer-valued auxiliary sum, include a local equality/integrality claim before declaring a normalized quotient is an integer.
  22. For converse directions, preserve the direction of implication explicitly: state the assumed target-side condition, prove each needed local vanishing or divisibility claim, then assemble the original condition.
  23. Treat stated "Fact", "Claim", or "Observation" blocks in the source as lemma-module candidates when they are proved and reused.
  24. For divisibility transitivity through a factorization, expose each bridge claim, such as A+B divides A^2-B^2 and A^2-B^2 divides A^(2m)-B^(2m).
  25. When a quotient is introduced by divisibility, thread it as an explicit integer witness before using it in later products or sums.
  26. For telescoping sums, separate endpoint terms, zero terms, and any reduced summation range before the final aggregation.
  27. If the source proof appears flawed, keep the suspect theorem or step isolated as its own claim or lemma module rather than repairing it with a different argument.
  28. In geometry proofs using directed-segment power equations, include a coordinate-translation claim mapping each directed segment to the algebraic coordinate difference.
  29. Before canceling or dividing by a geometric coordinate factor, add an explicit nonzero or distinctness claim when the source geometry supplies one.
  30. When a contradiction uses the existence of at least one root, state the relevant degree/root-existence fact explicitly before concluding that "no real roots" contradicts "all roots are real".
  31. For coefficient or index descent arguments, state the repeated propagation step with the adjacent hypotheses needed at each iteration.
  32. For elementary-symmetric polynomial arguments, expose coefficient-index translations as local claims before invoking coefficient-vanishing lemmas.
  33. For Rolle or interlacing arguments, mark the claim as standard calculus and state the derivative order, root count, and distinct-root conclusion being used.
  34. For finite-union or cardinality contradictions, separate the membership coverage claim from the per-fiber or per-set upper bound before aggregating cardinalities.
  35. If a contradiction assumption introduces a root set and later uses the constant term, record nonzero-root facts such as P(0) != 0 immediately.
  36. When converting a directed secant product to signed ordinary lengths, include a local betweenness/sign claim before the algebraic substitution.
  37. If a source proof appends a consistency check after the final theorem, mark it as nonessential or place it after final assembly so it is not a hidden dependency.
  38. For triangulation dual-tree proofs, record that dual edges correspond to diagonals and that component vertex counts match triangle counts in polygon parts.
  39. For sequential tree cuts, state that the component receiving the second cut remains a tree with the needed degree bound and that the second edge is distinct from the first.
  40. For CRT or product-modulus period decompositions, expose cross-prime lcm valuation issues as local claims before asserting that the valuation of a global period equals the valuation of a matching prime-power period.
  41. If a source proof corrects itself midstream, preserve both the mistaken or suspect claim and the corrected claim when needed, and make later dependencies point only to the corrected source-faithful statement.
  42. For base-expansion proofs, prove the exact digit count and valid digit-index range before applying digit-extraction formulas.
  43. When a digit argument uses quotient/remainder variables, declare least-nonnegative remainders explicitly and prove parity or lower-bound facts before using them.
  44. Keep strict threshold inequalities through floor-bound steps when the final target is a strict inequality such as a_j > d.
  45. For constructive polynomial examples, separate parameter-search motivation from final direct verification of the displayed polynomials.
  46. Degree claims for constructed polynomials should state that the relevant leading coefficients are nonzero.
  47. For polynomial identities involving composition, introduce reusable intermediate identities when they make both sides checkable for an arbitrary variable.
  48. If a positional-numeral proof expands a rational X and then multiplies by b^k, include an explicit claim that the resulting finite digit string is the base-b representation of the target integer.
  49. Separate digit-validity claims such as 0 <= c_i < b from digit lower-bound claims when both are present.

  51. When eventual constancy passes to a scaled auxiliary sequence, declare the scaled eventual value and its integrality explicitly, such as a_n = g b_n and b_n = K.
  52. In divisibility contradictions using selected subsequences, separate "the large divisor divides the subsequence terms" from "eventual constancy forces the same divisor to divide the limiting value".
  53. If a geometry proof resolves an ambiguous interpretation of tangent, secant, or intersection objects, isolate the chosen interpretation before using it; discarded interpretations should not become dependencies.

  55. For signed product cyclicity claims, state both magnitude equality and the side/order information needed to determine signs.

  58. If a preliminary reduction may solve the problem immediately, state an "if not already solved" branch before invoking the general construction.
  59. When a game rule is converted into a named graph such as a Gabriel graph, include a dedicated equivalence or reduction claim before proving graph properties.
  60. In geometric graph planarity proofs, isolate geometric facts such as "crossing segments form a convex quadrilateral" before applying angle or planarity arguments.
  61. For connectivity proofs by infimum, separate the disconnected partition, infimum-approaching sequence, witness non-edge, membership case split, and final limit contradiction.

  63. In strict geometric graph arguments, preserve strict inequalities from conditions such as exterior of a closed disk or distance > 1 through the final contradiction.
  64. For auxiliary-polynomial substitutions such as R(x) = Q(x)-x-1, include a claim translating the final auxiliary identity back to the original variables or functional equation.
  65. When source proofs say "by construction" for polynomial witnesses, expand the construction into local identities for both sides of the target equation.

  67. In k-subset family arguments, separate the combinatorial setup of two distinct k-subsets of a (k+1)-set from the algebraic consequence used afterward.

  72. When a ceiling or complementary sum is introduced, include a local equivalence claim relating it back to the original floor/fractional-part expression.
  73. Avoid overfitting to earlier examples of the same problem; preserve the proof strategy actually present in the current source text.
  74. For Gaussian-integer UFD arguments, state coprimality of conjugate factors, unit absorption, norm relation, and coordinate comparison before extracting powers from products.

  122. For repeated operation intervals like steps 2 through T, explicitly compute the count as T-1 and handle the smallest T allowed by the proof.

Few-shot example 1: converting a skeleton definition

Skeleton:
  impl3:
  k is a positive integer,
  n is a positive integer
  =>
  (A_n = k <=> s_k <= n < s_{k+1}).

Verifier format:
  claim C1:
  check_type: definition
  variables:
    k is a positive integer.
    n is a positive integer.
  hypotheses:
    A_n is the number of elements of S less than or equal to n.
    s_1 < s_2 < s_3 < ... are the elements of S in increasing order.
  allowed_references:
    none
  conclusion:
    A_n = k if and only if s_k <= n < s_{k+1}.

Few-shot example 3: global context vs claim

Bad global context:
  R(N) = O(N^(1/3)).
  There are infinitely many m such that J_m contains no perfect power.

Good global context:
  R(x) denotes the number of non-square perfect powers less than or equal to x.
  For each positive integer m, J_m denotes (m^2, (m+1)^2).

Good claims:
  claim C12:
  check_type: asymptotic estimate
  variables:
    N is a real number with N >= 2.
  hypotheses:
    R(N) <= N^(1/3) + (log_2(N)) N^(1/5).
  allowed_references:
    none
  conclusion:
    R(N) = O(N^(1/3)).

  claim C17:
  check_type: infinitude
  variables:
    none
  hypotheses:
    for every positive integer X, the number of m with 1 <= m <= X and J_m empty of non-square perfect powers is X - O(X^(2/3)).
  allowed_references:
    none
  conclusion:
    there are infinitely many positive integers m such that J_m contains no perfect power.


Few-shot example 4: final infinitude bridge

Skeleton:
  impl25:
  there are infinitely many positive integers k such that for each such k there exists a positive integer n with A_n = k and A_n divides n+2024
  =>
  there are infinitely many positive integers n such that A_n divides n+2024.

Verifier format:
  claim C25:
  check_type: infinitude
  variables:
    none
  hypotheses:
    there are infinitely many positive integers k such that for each such k there exists a positive integer n with A_n = k and A_n divides n+2024.
    a fixed positive integer n has exactly one value of A_n.
  allowed_references:
    none
  conclusion:
    there are infinitely many positive integers n such that A_n divides n+2024.


Few-shot example 5: lemma module and later instantiation

Skeleton:
  lemma L1_tree_partition:
  statement:
  H is a tree with M vertices and maximum degree at most 3,
  K is a positive integer,
  M >= 3K-1
  =>
  there exists an edge e of H such that removing e splits H into two components each having at least K vertices.

  proof:
  L1.impl1, L1.impl2, L1.impl3, L1.impl4.

  exports:
  L1.statement.

  impl_main:
  G is a tree with 18n vertices and maximum degree at most 3,
  K_1 = 6n,
  18n >= 3K_1-1,
  L1.statement instantiated with H=G, M=18n, K=K_1
  =>
  there exists an edge e_1 of G such that removing e_1 splits G into two components each having at least 6n vertices.

Verifier format:
  lemma L1_tree_partition:
  statement:
    For every tree H with M vertices and maximum degree at most 3, and every integer K >= 1, if M >= 3K-1, then there exists an edge e of H such that removing e splits H into two components each having at least K vertices.
  dependencies:
    none
  proof_claims:
    claim L1.C1:
    check_type: contradiction setup
    variables:
      H is a tree with M vertices.
      K is an integer with K >= 1.
    hypotheses:
      M >= 3K-1.
      for contradiction, no edge e of H splits H into two components each having at least K vertices.
    allowed_references:
      none
    conclusion:
      every edge e of H has at least one component with at most K-1 vertices after removing e.

    intermediate claims L1.C2, L1.C3, and L1.C4 are omitted from this few-shot snippet, but a real output should include every claim referenced below.

    claim L1.final:
    check_type: final assembly
    variables:
      H is a tree with M vertices and maximum degree at most 3.
      K is an integer with K >= 1.
    hypotheses:
      M >= 3K-1.
    allowed_references:
      - ref: L1.C1
        instantiation: H = current H; K = current K
      - ref: L1.C2
        instantiation: M = current M; K = current K
      - ref: L1.C3
        instantiation: H = current H; M = current M; K = current K
      - ref: L1.C4
        instantiation: H = current H; K = current K
    conclusion:
      there exists an edge e of H such that removing e splits H into two components each having at least K vertices.
  exports:
    L1.statement.

  claim C_first_split:
  check_type: lemma instantiation
  variables:
    none
  hypotheses:
